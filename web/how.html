Why the textbook is wrong here

Three surprises from measuring

1 · The small model was not cheap

Textbooks assume the draft costs ~5% of the target. Here each forward pass launched ~940 GPU kernels at ~13 µs each on Windows, so the 0.5B draft cost roughly two-thirds as much as the 7B.

2 · CUDA graphs changed the game

Replaying a recorded graph removes the launch overhead: r fell to 0.248 and the baseline itself got 1.85× faster.

3 · Verify cost is a staircase, not a line

Switch the explorer to Measured: verifying 4 tokens costs almost as much as 13. That is why γ=3 dips in every domain.

Why the output cannot change

The target computes its own choice at every position in the verify pass. A draft token is kept only if it equals that choice. At the first disagreement the target's choice is used and the rest is discarded. So every kept token is exactly what the target alone would have produced.

⚖️
The one exception: ties. When the target's top two tokens score identically in 16-bit floats, even plain decoding's pick depends on rounding order. That explains every mismatch we could reproduce. What is an fp16 tie?
